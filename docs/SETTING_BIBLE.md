# Labyrinth of the Dragon — Setting Bible

An original dark-fantasy world. Everything here is original to this project;
no characters, places, or plot are drawn from any existing published work.

---

## The Premise

The world was **Aetheria**, and it was whole. Sorcery and machinery were the
same craft — mages bound spirits into iron, and iron carried magic like blood
carries heat. Then the **Concordance**, the pact that held the five elemental
crowns in balance, was broken. Nobody agrees on who broke it.

What followed is called **the Ruin**: not a war, but a slow unraveling. The sky
turned the color of a bruise. The sea rose in places and vanished in others.
Cities that had stood for a thousand years became the Labyrinth — a tangle of
collapsed streets and impossible architecture that rearranges itself when no
one is looking.

At the center of it all is the **Dragon**. Not a beast — a thing older than
beasts, sleeping under the Labyrinth's deepest floor. It dreams, and its dreams
are what warp the corridors. The dragon's **eyes** are the last thing anyone
sees in the deep places, and the first thing you see when the game begins.

You are a **Warden**: one of the last people who can still walk the Labyrinth
without being unmade by it. You do not remember volunteering.

## The Five Acts

| Act | Name | The Question It Asks |
|-----|------|---------------------|
| I | **The Waking Dark** | Why can I walk where others are unmade? |
| II | **The Iron Choir** | Who profits from the Ruin? |
| III | **The Sunken Crown** | What did we build the Labyrinth *over*? |
| IV | **The Nameless Throne** | Who broke the Concordance, and does it matter now? |
| V | **The Dragon's Dream** | If the world is a dream, whose? |

Each act is ten chapters. Each act ends at a major threshold — a biome
transition in the tower, or a revelation that reframes the act before it.

## Factions

**The Ashen Order** — Wardens who stayed organized. They believe the Labyrinth
can be mapped, catalogued, and eventually *closed*. Rigid, bureaucratic, and
quietly terrified. They are the closest thing to allies in Act I.

**The Iron Choir** — Mage-smiths who sell enchanted weapons to everyone. They
publicly mourn the Ruin and privately got rich off it. Their foundries are the
only places in the world still manufacturing anything.

**The Tidebound** — Those who fled into the flooded lower levels and adapted.
They speak a dialect no one else understands and they do not come up.

**The Hollow** — People the Labyrinth unmade but did not kill. They walk. They
repeat the last thing they said before they were hollowed out. They are
dangerous mostly because they are pitiable, and you will hesitate.

**The Emberhand** — Cultists who want the Dragon to wake fully. They believe
the Ruin is a birth, not a death. They are not wrong, and that is the problem.

## Act I — The Waking Dark (Chapters 1–10)

You wake in the shallow Labyrinth with no memory and one possession: a torch
that will not go out. The Ashen Order finds you, does not trust you, and sends
you down anyway because they have no one else.

**Chapter beats:**

| # | Title | Beat |
|---|-------|------|
| 1 | **Cold Open** | Wake in a corridor. Learn to move. The torch lights itself. |
| 2 | **The First Hollow** | Meet something that used to be a person. It repeats itself. |
| 3 | **Stone and Silence** | First real dungeon floor. The Ashen Order watches from a ledge. |
| 4 | **The Ledger** | Find a Warden's journal. The handwriting is yours. |
| 5 | **Ash Gate** | Reach the Order's outpost. They let you in. Barely. |
| 6 | **What the Torch Knows** | The torch reacts to a door. Nobody explains why. |
| 7 | **The Second Name** | An NPC calls you by a name you do not recognize. |
| 8 | **Down** | The first descent. The architecture stops making sense. |
| 9 | **The Choir Below** | Hear the Iron Choir's forges through solid rock. |
| 10 | **The Eyes** | See the Dragon's eyes for the first time. Act I ends. |

## Chapter Structure (the template)

Every chapter in the game uses this shape, which the engine enforces:

1. **Entry text** — 1–3 textboxes setting the scene
2. **Objective** — what the player must do (usually: reach an exit)
3. **Encounters** — drawn from the floor's biome
4. **Discovery** — a chest, a sign, or an NPC that advances the plot
5. **Exit text** — 1–2 textboxes, sometimes ending on a question

Chapters 1–10 are Act I. The numbering is global (1–50), so Chapter 11 opens
Act II.

---

## Naming Conventions

- **Places**: plain, worn-down nouns — Ash Gate, the Ledger, Down.
- **People**: single names, no titles — Vessel, Corr, Marn, Ash.
- **The Dragon**: never named. It is "the Dragon," "the eyes," "the dreamer."
- **The Labyrinth**: capitalized, always. It is a proper noun and a character.

## What This Bible Deliberately Does Not Contain

- No characters from any published work
- No place names from any published work
- No plot beats lifted from any published work

The *genre* — heavy-metal dark fantasy, ruined kingdoms, demon lords, a world
wounded by sorcery — is not protectable and is shared by hundreds of works.
The specifics above are original to this project.
